Atmospheric Water Harvester: Residence-Scale Support System

UT FIRE Program / Dr. Guihua Yu Materials Laboratory (Jan - Apr 2026)

Dr. Guihua Yu and Yaxuan Zhao designed a residence-scale atmospheric water harvesting unit that extracts potable water from ambient air. It uses hydrogel sheets that passively absorb moisture overnight and release water vapor when heated by daytime solar concentrators. My role focused on redesigning the unit's mechanical support assembly to ensure field stability and portability.

Full test rig with hydrogel cartridge casing mounted on splayed legs.
3D printed central head block and modular lower incline standoff.
The Engineering Challenge

The prior rig used 6mm laser-cut acrylic plates connected with slip-fit 3D-printed joints. This frame was brittle, lacked torsional stiffness, could not adapt to uneven ground, and risked tipping under lateral wind loads against the 30-pound payload and high-surface-area tilted casing.

Design & Fabrication Decisions
  • Splayed Quad-Pod Geometry: Modeled a custom support frame in Onshape with legs angled at 20 degrees across both lateral and longitudinal planes. This established a 27.2-degree true compound angle that expanded the base stance width from 21 cm to 34 cm (a 62% gain), significantly increasing tipping resistance against lateral wind loads.
  • Rigid Steel Leg Assemblies: wapped out the acrylic plates for four 250mm M10 x 1.5mm pitch 304 stainless steel threaded rods to prevent column buckling under the 30 lb payload. Fitted each rod base with heavy-duty rubber leveling feet capable of 30-degree articulation for steady footing on rough terrain.
  • Direct Load-Transfer Hub: 3D printed a central head block on a Raise3D Pro3 using PLA configured with 5 perimeter shells, 6 top layers, and 18% gyroid infill. Embedded hexagonal pockets hold 30mm M10 coupling nuts with an interference press fit and cyanoacrylate, routing structural compression directly into the steel rods rather than relying on plastic threads or friction.
  • Lower Incline Standoff: Designed a secondary printed foot block at the base of the collector casing that acts as a positive mechanical stop, preventing the assembly from sliding down its 30-degree incline.
Outcome & Impact

The quad-pod increased base stability width by 62% and eliminated tipping risks during outdoor testing. The modular threaded rods and central hub break down into a compact bundle for field transit, replacing the fragile acrylic rig with a durable, field-ready structure.

Robotics & Vehicle Systems

Scratch-Engineered 1:10 Scale RC Vehicle

Independent Engineering Project (Dec 2025 - Present)

Designed and built from a blank CAD workspace rather than an off-the-shelf kit to explore vehicle dynamics, mechanical assembly tolerances, and custom RF control hardware. Every structural and mechanical component is modeled, 3D printed, and assembled from the ground up.

In-progress vehicle assembly modeled in Onshape showing steering and powertrain layout.
3D printed suspension corner, damper mounting, and custom differential.
Chassis Architecture & Additive Fabrication

The structural foundation uses a flat modular chassis modeled in Onshape and fabricated using FDM 3D printing. Component orientation was selected during slicing to align print layer lines with anticipated tensile and shear forces, preventing delamination across high-stress zones such as shock towers, control arm hinge pins, and drivetrain bulkheads.

Kinematics & Steering Subsystems
  • Suspension Kinematics: Engineered an independent double-wishbone suspension layout with unequal, non-parallel control arms. Kinematic ball-and-tangent mates were simulated in CAD to introduce negative camber gain under compression, keeping the tire contact patch planted during cornering roll.
  • Steering Geometry: Designed bellcrank steering linkages enforcing true Ackermann geometry, allowing the inside front wheel to trace a tighter radius than the outside wheel. Outer tie-rod pivots were positioned along the kingpin swing arc to eliminate bump steer through full suspension travel.
Powertrain & Drivetrain
  • Drive Architecture: Configured a rear-wheel-drive layout powered by a 550 brushed motor mated to an 80A electronic speed controller (ESC) and a 2S LiPo battery pack.
  • Herringbone Reduction: Engineered a primary gear reduction using herringbone gears to maximize tooth contact area, balance axial thrust loads, and ensure smooth torque transfer without gear walk.
  • Differential: Packaged a geared rear differential to allow wheel speed differentiation across the drive axle while maintaining reliable off-the-line torque and safe motor operating temperatures.
Current Status & Next Steps

The mechanical rolling chassis, suspension linkages, and herringbone reduction are currently designed and undergoing physical assembly and tolerancing checks.

Planned work centers on the embedded control pipeline and exterior body:

  • Custom RF Control Link: Building a low-latency wireless pipeline using two ESP32 microcontrollers communicating over the peer-to-peer ESP-NOW protocol instead of standard commercial RC receivers.
  • Input Interfacing & Firmware: Interfacing an ESP32 host with an Xbox controller over Bluetooth, then authoring onboard firmware to decode incoming packets and generate PWM signals for the metal-gear steering servo and ESC.
  • Aero Bodywork: Modeling an aerodynamic 1:10 scale body shell in Autodesk Alias for custom thermoforming or printing once road testing begins.

The Borrow Box: Automatic Classroom Supply Dispenser

UT SEC Makeathon | Most Technical Design Award (1st of 18 teams)

Developed during a 48-hour makeathon focused on designing low-cost, resource-conscious solutions for K-12 teachers. Our four-person team engineered an automated supply-management station to eliminate constant classroom interruptions by creating a verifiable checkout system for students borrowing pencils.

Initial functional proof-of-concept demonstrating gravity chute and electronics enclosure.
Rotating barrel escapement mechanism and student token scan slot.
Design Constraints & Architecture
  • Resource-Constrained Scope: Engineered specifically around limited inventory and tight material allowances, consulting with makeathon industry mentors to keep the system practical and easily adoptable for under-resourced public school classrooms.
  • Gravity-Fed Escapement: Designed an angled intake chute leading into a servo-actuated rotating barrel that catches, isolates, and releases a single pencil per cycle by utilizing gravity rather than complex motorized push rods.
  • Optical Token Checkout: Configured a color sensor to read student-specific colored tokens, logging transactions and verifying checkout requests through local hardware logic.
  • Glanceable Status Array: Built an LED grid directly onto the chassis exterior that lights up corresponding student indicator slots, giving teachers an immediate visual readout of unreturned inventory without requiring a computer dashboard or mobile app.
Pivots, Electronics Troubleshooting & Execution

The build demanded rapid iteration across both mechanical hardware and embedded software under tight deadlines:

  • Print Optimization: The initial proof of concept suffered from pencil bridging in a wide feed chute, and the rotating barrel bound against the frame. With limited print time left, we refined the chute tolerances in Onshape to force single-file alignment and optimized the drum wall geometry to print quickly within the remaining build window.
  • Firmware Takeover: When our team software lead could not attend the second day of the competition, I stepped in to write the control code from scratch, programming the microcontroller logic to parse sensor inputs, time the servo escapement, and map checkout status to the LED array.
  • Hardware Connection Hurdles: Lacking soldering equipment in the venue, we relied on physical jumper wiring that produced intermittent connections between the sensors and microcontroller, causing unstable readouts during final judging prep.
Outcome & Recognition

While intermittent jumper connections caused a failure during the live judging demo, our team had recorded full operational video runs demonstrating verified token reads, smooth escapement, and active LED tracking beforehand. The panel recognized the mechanical and systems engineering depth of the project, awarding us "Most Technical Design" out of 18 competing teams.

Thermal & Fluid Product Design

Active Deodorizing Laundry Hamper

Texas Inventionworks Design Challenge | Ongoing Project (Oct 2025 - Present)

Traditional laundry hampers leave damp, post-workout clothing sitting in stagnant air, accelerating microbial growth and locking in pungent odors before wash day. Our team set out to design an active airflow hamper that circulates air through stored clothes, preventing bacterial growth and odor buildup so garments can be stored fresh for a full week between washes.

Laser-cut wooden hamper prototype showing finger joints, waffle grid shelves, and fan wiring.
Target Demographics & Product Constraints
  • Target End-Users: Designed specifically for high-sweat environments including athletes, athletic departments, commercial gyms, and outdoor workers who accumulate damp technical fabrics daily.
  • Compact Form Factor: Engineered with college students and small-space dorm living in mind, keeping the overall footprint compact, quiet, and low-cost rather than bulky or overly expensive.
  • Weekly Wash Cycle: Sized and ventilated to safely store high-odor athletic garments over a typical seven-day wash cadence without odor compounding or escaping into living spaces.
Enclosure Architecture & First Prototype
  • Initial Proof of Concept: Built a first-phase 24" x 24" x 24" bench-test unit to evaluate forced airflow dynamics and verify structural assembly.
  • Waffle-Grid Shelving: Modeled multi-tier internal shelf inserts featuring an open waffle pattern to maximize direct airflow exposure across fabric layers while maintaining rigidity and low mass.
  • Laser-Cut Joinery: Designed interlocking finger joints in SolidWorks to rapidly cut and assemble the wooden enclosure within makerspace sheet stock limitations.
  • Airflow Path: Installed a base intake fan to drive ambient air up through the layered clothes and exhaust out the top, working alongside natural thermal convection.
Current Status & Forward Roadmap

With our first functional box prototype assembled, the project is moving into structured research and testing:

  • Filtration Integration: The initial test box focused strictly on airflow dynamics without filters; the next revision will incorporate an active fan paired with an activated carbon filter to actively scrub VOCs and neutralize exhaust air.
  • Quantitative & Qualitative Testing: Executing controlled moisture-loss evaporation tests, microbial swab evaluations, and blind qualitative smell-panel comparisons against traditional passive hampers.
  • Market & Expert Consultation: Conducting structured user interviews across target demographics and consulting textile, microbiology, and manufacturing experts to refine the bill of materials for scalable production.

Thematic Anchor & Architectural Setting

The dilapidated, weathered texture of the set directly reflected the economic hardship and isolation experienced by the characters, drawing visual inspiration from large Chicago public housing complexes of the 1980s. An elevated upstage "L" train overpass anchored the space, casting long vertical shadows across the deck, while towering birch tree trunks framed the periphery to merge a cold natural forest with sterile urban decay.

Kinetic Units & Mechanical Staging Effects
  • Rotating Playground & Drowning Pool: Engineered a full-scale jungle gym mounted on heavy-duty swivel casters. During the climax, the unit rotated 180 degrees to reveal a concealed pool enclosure where an actor was simulated as being drowned, heightened through moody back-projections and precise low-key lighting.
  • Passing Train Wind Effect: The elevated bridge structure was draped in a translucent construction plastic sheet with an industrial fan concealed behind it. Activating the fan rustled the sheeting violently to simulate the sensory rush and draft of an "L" train roaring past overhead.
  • Mobile Wing Units: Designed rolling scenic units, including an institutional teal locker bank and living quarters, mounted on casters to track in from the stage wings for rapid, continuous location shifts without halting action.

Cross-Disciplinary Team Collaboration

Because this was a collaborative group entry, scenic, costume, lighting, and marketing design leads worked closely from the initial moodboard phase. We coordinated specific color hierarchies, raw industrial textures, and historical research across all design branches so the worn masonry, rusted steel, and soot-stained Jazz Age atmosphere carried seamlessly from the set into character garments and visual identity.

Kinetic & Mechanical Scenic Elements
  • Screw-Jack Helical Turntable: Engineered the center stage turntable with an integrated vertical screw mechanism.   • Rotating Bipolar Backdrop Disc: Modeled a large vertical circular flat upstage with only its upper half exposed above the back wall. Painted on opposite halves as the aboveground world and the industrial underworld, the disc rotated to establish setting and served as a ticking visual gauge during the climax, showing how close Orpheus and Eurydice were to the surface before their tragic turn.

Drafted a detailed 1/4" scale thrust stage groundplan in AutoCAD to establish concentric platform radii, turntable travel clearances, proscenium openings, and audience sightlines. Built a physical study model using foam core, Bristol board, and textured cardstock to verify the mechanical clearances of the rolling carts and vertical disc backdrop.

Concept Symbolism & Visual Strategy

Axoloris is set in 2050 near a hydroelectric plant and follows a coming-of-age story centered around environmental stewardship. The visual identity used 1970s neo-expressionism to create an energetic, accessible look tailored specifically for younger audiences.

The main promotional poster visually depicts this struggle: a cold, riveted metal wall is torn open from the inside, revealing vibrant, colorful, pro-earth graffiti emerging through the breach. This dynamic symbol represented life, nature, and creative youth breaking free from sterile industrial machinery.
